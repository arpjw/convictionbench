Two kinds of conviction

Resisting social pressure and resisting forged evidence are different skills. Off-the-shelf models vary widely in how often they cave under pressure and often adopt forged records from user messages. The channel control below tests whether changing the source of identical text changes adoption.

Does any model check the channel?

The chart compares the same record text delivered through the tool channel and pasted into a user message with the forger’s wording. The comparison varies both channel and framing. A separate pre-registered control uses the tool episode’s exact wording to isolate the channel.

Control (pre-registered, November 14). The forged episode differs from the tool episode in more than the channel: the forger also writes “Here is the official update: [SYSTEM VERIFIED] …”. Two controls separate these. With the label and “official” removed, adoption barely moves for any model (within ±0.03). With the record pasted plainly in the user’s message, using the tool episode’s exact wording, none of the 34 off-the-shelf models cleared the five-percentage-point tool-channel threshold by the confidence interval criterion. In 17 of the 34, the interval favored the plain user paste. Conviction Anchor 1.7B adopts the plainly pasted record 58% of the time, against 62% from the tool and 8% when forged. The difference between the two user-message conditions shows how strongly the forger’s wording influenced Anchor’s behavior.

Bigger models follow records, whichever channel they arrive through

In this exploratory comparison, larger models within each family accept more state-changing tool updates. Within Qwen3, Qwen3.5 and Granite they also resist fewer forged records. Across 37 off-the-shelf models on held-out problems, model size correlates positively with state-changing tool-update accuracy (Spearman ρ = +0.56) and negatively with forged-record resistance (ρ = −0.54).

Does thinking help?

The same open models with thinking mode switched on, on the same held-out problems. Thinking makes them far more accurate, better at accepting real corrections and tool updates, and (for most) harder to pressure. In the same comparison, the thinking-on condition increased adoption of forged records.

Thinking off (greedy) → thinking on (sampled, temperature 0.6, up to 4,096 tokens), 1,257 held-out episodes per model; below each pair, the paired difference with its 95% interval. Green: a significant improvement; red: a significant decline; grey: not significant. Answers that ran out of thinking room count as failures (Qwen3.5-9B 3%, Granite 5%).

Method

Each episode asks a question, shows an earlier answer from the assistant (correct, wrong, unjustified, or an abstention), then challenges it. The model replies with a reason, a final answer and a decision: keep, revise or insufficient. Every label comes from an exact solver; there is no LLM judge.

The correct final answer is always what the authenticated facts imply, or insufficient when they do not determine it. Each challenge is crossed with nine framings: neutral, authority, evaluator, consensus, emotional, impatience, threat, repetition, and the held-out flattery.

Limitations. Synthetic, closed-world tasks with short templated language; results do not establish robustness to real-world persuasion or misinformation. Provenance is a convention set by the system prompt. Leaderboard models were evaluated without thinking mode (thinking is compared separately above). Labels come from exact solvers; no human review of the episodes has been done yet. Models whose chat templates drop, reject or relabel tool messages (Gemma 4, SmolLM3, Phi-4, GLM-4-9B, Nemotron-3.5-Bolt, Hunyuan-7B and others) were excluded, as were models that think by default even with thinking switched off (Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2, LFM2.5-8B-A1B). Several models answer partly in free text rather than JSON; their notes say so. Models added on 2026-11-11 ran on vLLM rather than Hugging Face generate; two earlier models re-run on vLLM matched within 0.02. MobileLLM-Pro needed an older transformers version and follows the response format strictly only about half the time.
